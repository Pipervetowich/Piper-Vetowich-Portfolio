Brighten Your Being

A website for a wellness retreat, built on a full design system with an accessibility-first approach.

Add hero image or site screenshot

Overview

As the freelance designer and developer on Brighten Your Being, I took the project from a first client conversation to a live, WCAG AA–audited site over a six-week engagement — owning discovery, visual design, front-end build, and accessibility testing myself.

Because the client's brand promise is calm and inclusivity, I treated accessibility as a design requirement from day one, not a pass at the end — and built the whole site on a reusable design system so the client could keep extending it after launch.

The problem

The client's existing site didn't reflect the calm, grounded feeling of the retreat itself, and hadn't been checked against accessibility standards — a priority for a wellness brand serving a wide range of guests.

The goal was a site that felt as restorative to browse as the retreat itself is to visit, built on a design system the client could keep extending long after launch.

My role

Working solo and directly with the client, I was responsible for:

  • Led discovery — worked with the client to define the tone, color, and typography that matched the retreat's in-person atmosphere.
  • Built the design system — a reusable component library covering type scale, color tokens, and layout patterns.
  • Ran the accessibility audit — tested contrast, keyboard navigation, and screen-reader flow against WCAG guidelines and fixed every issue found.
  • Shipped the build — hand-coded the site in HTML/CSS and deployed it to Netlify.

Reflection

Results & impact

  • Live, accessible site: Shipped a WCAG AA–audited site that reflects the retreat's calm, grounded brand.
  • Reusable design system: Gave the client a component library they can extend without breaking consistency.
  • End-to-end delivery: Owned discovery through deployment solo, on a six-week timeline.

Key learnings

  • Accessibility is easier built in than bolted on: Designing with contrast and focus states in mind from the start meant far fewer fixes at audit time.
  • A design system pays off fastest for solo work: Reusable components let one person credibly deliver a multi-page, on-brand site in six weeks.
  • Client education is part of the deliverable: A system is only as durable as the client's ability to use it after handoff.
